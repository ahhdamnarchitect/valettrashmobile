## The economics — run these on the prospect's own numbers

The property's business case is the margin between what they collect from residents and what they
pay Reggie. Reggie must ask **what they charge residents** before he quotes; without that number
the pitch has no math.

### Example: 200 units, 90% occupancy (180 billable doors)

| | Resident charge $25 | Resident charge $30 | Resident charge $35 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Property collects | $4,500 | $5,400 | $6,300 |
| Pays RLV @ $18 | $3,240 | $3,240 | $3,240 |
| **Property keeps** | **$1,260/mo** | **$2,160/mo** | **$3,060/mo** |

| **Property keeps** | **$1,800/mo** | **$2,700/mo** | **$3,600/mo** |

**Read the table before every call.** At a $25 resident charge the month-to-month rate is thin and
a national quoting $11 gives them nearly double the margin — *that is a disqualify or a 36-month
conversation, not a fight.* At $30–35 the math works comfortably.

## Competitive position — he is a PREMIUM vendor

Market rate for valet trash is **$8–15/door** to the property ($12–18 for small communities).
At $15–18 Reggie sits at the **top of the range**. He cannot win on price and must never try.

Against a national at ~$11/door on a 180-door property he is asking for roughly **$1,200/month
more**. Everything in the script exists to justify that gap. The four things that justify it:

1. **Month-to-month with a 30-day out.** No national offers this. This is the single strongest
   asset in the entire offer — see *Contract structure*.
2. **W-2 employees, not 1099 contractors.** Much of this industry runs on contractors, which is a
   real liability exposure for a property and an active misclassification issue in the category.
   His people are on his payroll, under his workers comp.
3. **Proof, not promises.** The app produces a timestamped nightly run log with a completion
   percentage, exportable to CSV per property, plus photo-evidence violation reports. The nationals
   send a monthly summary email at best.
4. **Local owner-operator.** Reggie answers his own phone. Not a call center in Tampa.

**Target properties currently being burned by a national.** Against a happy incumbent at $11 he
loses. Against a manager eating resident complaints every week, $6/door is cheap.

## ICP — who to go after, in priority order

### Tier 1 — no valet trash at all *(best target, lead here)*
A property with no valet trash is not a vendor swap, it is a **new profit center**. No incumbent to
displace, no contract to wait out, no transition risk. 200 units at $30/resident is roughly
**$2,700/month the property is not currently making**. This is the easiest yes available and it
should be the bulk of early prospecting.

Where they are: **Class B / Class C garden-style**, roughly **100–350 units**, owned by independents
or small local management companies. Valet trash penetration is near-total in Class A and spotty in
B/C — that gap is the whole opportunity.

### Tier 2 — has valet trash, unhappy with it
The documented complaint pattern against the nationals is missed nights, inconsistent times, bags
left in breezeways, spills, and unresponsive support — and the **on-site manager eats the resident
complaints**, not the vendor. Play: capture the contract expiry date and the notice window, then own
the renewal.

### Tier 3 — has valet trash, happy with it
Get the expiry date, leave a card, calendar it. Do not fight.

### Hard disqualifiers — stop, do not spend a month on these
- **Managed by a national with a preferred-vendor agreement** (Greystar, RPM Living, Cortland,
  Lincoln, Willow Bridge, BH, RangeWater and similar). The on-site manager *literally cannot* buy
  from him. Exit politely.
- **Under 100 units** — the route economics don't carry the labor.
- **Property charging residents $25 or less** and shopping purely on price.

## Contract structure — this is the sales weapon

Two templates exist. They are the same document except for term and exit.

### Month-to-month
- Term: month to month
- **Either party may terminate for any reason on 30 days written notice** (certified mail)
- **No early termination fee. No liquidated damages.**

### 36-month
- 36-month initial term, then auto-rolls **month to month**
- No termination during the initial term except by written agreement or under the default clause
- **Liquidated damages:** 6× average monthly fee if terminated with >12 months remaining;
  45% of average monthly fee per remaining month if <12 months remaining
- Rate may change in the subsequent term, capped at **10% per 12 months**, 30 days notice

### Terms common to both
- **Default + 15-day cure** — and the clause explicitly names *"Contractor's failure to provide the
  valet services in a professional and competent manner"* as a default. **Reggie should point at
  this.** It is a service guarantee already written into his own paper.
- Indemnity cap: greater of available insurance proceeds or 12× average monthly fee
- Assignment requires consent, not unreasonably withheld — survives an ownership change
- Texas law; venue in the county where the property sits

**Why the month-to-month wins deals:** every national hands over a 36–60 month contract with
liquidated damages. A 30-day out with no fee is an offer none of them will match, and it is the
direct answer to the hardest objection Reggie faces — *"you're new, why would I risk my property
on you."*
