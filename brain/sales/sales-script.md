# Sales Script — Valet Trash / Apartment Communities (DFW)

> Discovery + book-the-walk script for **Relaxed Living Valet**. Voice: plain, local, owner-operator.
> We sell a **property's profit center and a clean breezeway** — never "an app."
> No guarantees we can't defend. Numbers on **their** figures, never ours.
>
> **Pricing:** **$18/door month-to-month** · **$15/door on 36 months** · recycling +$7/door (2-yr min)
> · lost or damaged can **$40** to the property. Full terms: `offer.md`.
>
> **v1 (2026-08-21).** Two spines — the **walk-in** and the **cold call** — because Reggie is doing
> both. They converge at §2.
>
> **Memorize only §1A, §1B, §2, §5, §7 and objections A/B/E/G.** Everything marked *(reference)* is
> for reading before you go and reviewing after — not for reciting.

---

## HOW TO RUN THIS — read every time

The words below work. What sinks a call is *delivery*. Six rules:

1. **3-sentence cap.** Make your point in three sentences or fewer, then **stop and let them talk.**
   Monologues manufacture objections. "That sounds like a lot" is what somebody says right after you
   *gave* them a lot. Under resistance, go **shorter, never longer.**
2. **Kill the filler.** Every "uh / um / you know" reads as unsure. Replace it with a **half-second of
   silence.** Silence sounds like confidence.
3. **Let the number land.** After you say their margin out loud, **pause.** Do not chase it with
   "so that's pretty good, right?" The silence closes harder than the question.
4. **"How long have you been doing this?" is a FEAR question, not a resume request.** They're scared
   of a vendor who disappears. Answer with **the month-to-month and an easy exit, in two sentences.
   Never oversell your history.** (Objection E.)
5. **Never quote before you know what they charge residents.** Their margin *is* the pitch. Quoting
   into a vacuum is how you get "that's more than the other guy" with nothing to answer it.
6. **Win condition = a 15-minute sit-down with the person who signs, or the incumbent's contract
   end date.** You do NOT close on the first contact. Nobody signs a vendor on a walk-in.
   **These also count as wins:** the Community Manager's name and email from a leasing agent; a
   scheduled callback; a clean "we're managed by Greystar" that saves you a month; and an honest
   "you're not a fit." A clean exit is a win. The only real losses are folding, bluffing, and
   talking past a no.

> Bonus: **use their own words.** If they say "our current guys are… fine, mostly" — pounce.
> "That 'mostly' is what I'd want to hear more about."

## 0. Before you walk in or dial — 60 seconds of prep *(reference)*

Know these three things before you open your mouth:

- **Unit count.** Apartments.com, the property website, or the sign out front. This determines
  whether they're worth your time at all (under 100 units, walk).
- **Who manages it.** The footer of their website, or the sign. **If it's a national with a
  preferred-vendor deal — Greystar, RPM Living, Cortland, Lincoln, Willow Bridge, BH, RangeWater —
  the on-site manager cannot buy from you.** Don't burn the visit.
- **Do they already have valet trash?** Google reviews mention it constantly, usually to complain.
  Their fee schedule page will list it. **A property with none is your best prospect** — that's a
  new profit center, not a vendor fight.

One observed detail beats any generic pitch. If their reviews say "valet trash never shows," you
walk in already knowing where the wound is.

## 0.5 TRIAGE — the first 10 seconds decide which script you're running *(reference)*

| What you see / hear | You're in | Go to |
|---|---|---|
| Young agent at the front desk, tours on the board | **Leasing agent — not the buyer** | §1C |
| "I'm the community manager, what's this about?" | **Champion, maybe not the signer** | §1A |
| "You'd have to talk to our regional" | **Real authority block** | §1D |
| "We're a Greystar property" (or similar) | **Hard disqualify** | Exits |
| "We already have valet trash" | **Tier 2 or 3** | §3B |
| "We don't do valet trash here" | **TIER 1 — your best case** | §3A |
| Phone, gatekeeper screening | **Cold call** | §1B |

**The rule under all of it: match their time, not your script.** A leasing office at 4:45pm on the
first of the month is not going to give you ten minutes. Read the room and book a better one.

## 1. Openers

### 1A. THE WALK-IN — memorize

Leasing offices are open, public, and expect walk-ins. This is your highest-yield channel. Dress
like a vendor, not a salesman. Ask for nothing on the first visit except a name and a calendar slot.

> "Morning — I'm Reggie, I own Relaxed Living Valet, we're local here in DFW. I'll be quick and then
> I'll get out of your way. We do doorstep trash pickup for apartment communities. Do you all have
> valet trash here right now?"

**[Stop. Their answer routes the entire rest of the conversation — §3A or §3B.]**

*(That question does three jobs at once: it qualifies them, it's easy to answer, and it doesn't
sound like a pitch.)*

### 1B. THE COLD CALL — memorize

> "Hi, this is Reggie with Relaxed Living Valet — we're local, here in DFW. I'll be quick. We do
> doorstep trash pickup for apartment communities and I'm calling the ones in [city] that either
> don't have it or aren't thrilled with who they've got. Which one are you?"

*(Yes → §2. "Who is this / not interested / send me an email" → Objection G.)*

### 1C. A LEASING AGENT ANSWERS *(reference)*

Do not pitch. They cannot buy, and pitching them wastes your one visit and marks you as somebody who
doesn't know how a property works. Twenty seconds, get the name, leave clean.

> "Ah — you're probably not who I need, my fault. Quick one, though: do you all have valet trash
> here? …Got it. And who's your community manager? …And is she usually around mornings or
> afternoons? …Perfect. I'll leave you my card — I'm Reggie, tell her I'll swing back Tuesday."

**Then actually come back Tuesday.** Showing up when you said you would is the first proof you'll
ever give them, and it costs nothing.

### 1D. "You'd have to talk to our regional" *(reference)*

That is not a brush-off, it's the truth — regionals sign ancillary contracts. **Never send the
on-site manager off to relay your pitch.** They'll do it badly and come back with a no.

> "Makes sense — that's usually how it works. Two things then: what's their name, and would you put
> in a good word if I reach out? …And honestly, you're the one who'd deal with this day to day. If I
> could show you in ten minutes what your residents would actually see, would that help you when
> they ask you about it?"

*(Convert the on-site manager into your internal advocate. A champion inside beats a cold email to
a regional every time.)*

## 2. THE TWO QUESTIONS THAT QUALIFY — memorize

Everything hinges on these. Ask them early, ask them plainly, write down the answers.

> **1. "How many units do you have here?"**
> **2. "And what do you charge residents for valet trash — or what were you thinking of charging?"**

**Do not quote a price until you have question 2 answered.** Their margin is the entire pitch. If
they charge $35 and you're $18, they keep $17 a door and the conversation is easy. If they charge
$25, it's thin and you're heading for a 36-month conversation or a walk.

**If they don't know** *(common with a leasing agent, or a property that's never had it)*:
> "No problem — most places around here run $25 to $35 a month. Somewhere in there is normal."

**Then the third question, only for Tier 2:**
> **3. "Who's doing it for you now, and when's that contract up?"**

*(The expiry date is the single most valuable thing you can leave with. Write it down. Calendar it.
Most managers miss their own 30-to-90-day notice window and roll into another year — being the guy
who calls sixty days before is how you win a property you can't win today.)*

## 3. DISCOVERY — branch on what they've got

### 3A. THEY HAVE NO VALET TRASH — your best case *(memorize the math line)*

This is not a vendor swap. It's **money they aren't currently making.** Frame it that way and don't
mention competitors at all.

> "So right now that's just revenue you're not collecting. Most properties your size charge residents
> thirty a month for this, keep the difference over what they pay me, and it drops straight to the
> bottom line. On [X] units that's real money — and residents actually like it, which is rare for
> anything you add to a ledger."

**Then the math, out loud, once, on their numbers:**
> "Say you charge thirty. [X] doors is $[X × 30] a month coming in. You'd pay me eighteen a door —
> $[X × 18]. You'd keep about **$[difference] a month** you're not making today."

**[Stop talking. Let it sit.]**

Then the second reason, which matters more to them than money on a bad week:
> "The other half is your breezeways. Right now your residents are hauling bags to the compactor —
> or not, and leaving them by the door. Your maintenance guys are picking up what doesn't make it."

**Discovery to run once they're engaged:**
1. "Who handles it when a resident leaves trash out now?"
2. "How much time do your maintenance guys spend on that?"
3. "Have you had code or fire-marshal issues with the breezeways?"
4. "Is adding a fee something you can do at renewal, or does that have to go through corporate?"

### 3B. THEY ALREADY HAVE VALET TRASH — go fishing *(reference)*

Do not attack the incumbent. **Ask questions and let them complain.** If they don't complain,
they're Tier 3 and you're getting an expiry date and leaving.

> "How's that going for you? …Do they show up every night they're supposed to? …When something goes
> wrong, how fast do you hear back from them? …And when a resident's mad about it, who do they come
> to — you, or them?"

**[The last question is the one. The answer is almost always "me."]**

Then:
> "That's the part that always gets me. You're paying somebody, and you're *still* the one absorbing
> it when it goes sideways."

**If they're genuinely happy** *(Tier 3)* — respect it, get the date, leave:
> "Good, that's how it should be. Do me one favor — when's that contract up? …I'll check back with
> you about sixty days out. Not to pester you, just so you've got a second number when it's renewal
> time. Here's my card."

## 4. THE PROOF — what makes you different, and it isn't a feature list *(memorize the first line)*

Only run this once they've engaged. It's the answer to "why you instead of the big guys," and it is
**two things, not five.**

> "Two things. One — my guys are W-2 employees on my payroll and my workers comp, not contractors
> off an app. Two — you get a log every night with timestamps showing exactly what got picked up and
> what didn't, and you can export it. Most companies send you a summary once a month, if that."

**[STOP. Do not keep going. Do not open the app unless they ask.]**

**If they ask to see it** — that's a buying signal, show them on the phone or an iPad:
- The nightly run log with timestamps and a completion percentage
- Photo evidence when a resident leaves something they shouldn't
- What the resident sees — including that they can request a comeback pickup themselves instead
  of calling your office

## 5. THE CLOSE — the month-to-month is the whole weapon — MEMORIZE

This is the most important paragraph in this document. It is the answer to every version of "you're
new and I don't know you," and no national competitor will match it.

> "Here's how I'd do it. Month to month — thirty days notice, any reason, no fee. Every other company
> is going to hand you a three-year contract with a cancellation penalty in it. I'd rather earn the
> three years than trap you into them. If we miss nights, you fire me and you're out nothing."

**[Stop talking. Let it sit.]**

**If they want the better rate**, that's when the long term comes out — never before:
> "If you want to lock a rate, I'll do fifteen a door on thirty-six months instead of eighteen. But
> I'd honestly start month to month and let me prove it first. Do it backwards and you're just
> taking my word for it, same as the last guy."

*(Offering the discount you could have led with — and then talking them toward the safer option —
is the most trust you will build in the entire conversation. Do it.)*

## 6. WHAT YOU ACTUALLY DO — only if they ask, keep it to 3 lines *(reference)*

> "Five days a week, my guy comes down the breezeways and takes the bag from outside their door to
> the compactor. Every unit gets a container from me. Residents put it out during the window, we're
> gone before anybody's up."

Nights vs mornings — **their choice, say so**:
> "Sunday through Thursday evenings, or Monday through Friday mornings — whichever fits your
> property. If we do mornings, residents set out that morning, not the night before, so you don't
> have bags sitting out overnight."

---

## 7. LOCK THE NEXT STEP AND LEAVE — memorize

The second they give you a name, a date, or a yes, **stop selling.** Confirm the next step in one
breath and go. No trailing off, no "well, it was great meeting you."

**After a walk-in that went well:**
> "Perfect. I'll come back Thursday at ten with numbers for your unit count and you can tell me if
> it's worth taking to your regional. Thursday at ten work? …Great. What's the best email for you?"
> **[leave]**

**After you get a contract expiry date:**
> "Got it — March. I'll check in with you in January, about sixty days out, so you've got a second
> option in front of you before it renews. That's all I want. Take care." **[leave]**

**After a yes:**
> "Great — I'll get you a contract and a certificate of insurance today. I need your unit count and
> the name it goes in. Give me two weeks to get containers on site and we can start [date]."
> *(Then: send the COI and contract the same day. Same day. That's the first promise you keep.)*

## OBJECTION CHEAT SHEET — say these, don't improvise (all ≤3 sentences)

**A. "How much is it?"** *(give the number straight — never dodge price)*
> "Eighteen a door, month to month. Fifteen if you lock thirty-six months. But before that lands as
> high or low — what are you charging residents? That's the number that decides whether this is
> worth doing at all."

**B. "That's higher than the quote I got from [national]."** *(hold value — never discount on the spot)*
> "It probably is. They're cheaper because they're running contractors and a call center in another
> state, and you're the one who eats it when your residents are mad. You're paying me a few dollars a
> door to not deal with that — and month to month, so if I'm wrong you're out in thirty days."

**C. "We're under contract."**
> "Figured you might be. When's it up? …And do you know your notice window — a lot of these auto-renew
> if you miss it by thirty days. I'll check back sixty days out, that's all."

**D. "We already have valet trash and it's fine."**
> "Good, honestly. Two questions and I'll get out of your hair: do they hit every night they're
> supposed to, and when a resident's unhappy, does that come to you or to them?"

**E. "How long have you been doing this?" / "How many properties do you have?"** *(FEAR question — don't oversell)*
> "I'll be straight with you — you'd be one of my first. That's exactly why I'm offering month to
> month with no cancellation fee, and why you get my cell instead of a 1-800 number. A year from now
> I won't be able to give you that deal." **[STOP. Do not list features to compensate.]**

**F. "Residents will complain about another fee."**
> "Some will, at first — same as any amenity. The ones who use it stop complaining fast, because the
> alternative is walking a bag across the property at ten at night. And I'd rather you put it on new
> leases at renewal than force it on everybody mid-lease."

**G. "Not interested." / "Just email me something."**
> "Fair enough. Can I leave a card and get your name at least? …And one question before I go — do you
> all have valet trash here now? …Appreciate it. I'll follow up in a couple months."

**H. "I'd have to run it by my regional / corporate."** *(real authority block — do NOT sell the non-buyer)*
> "That's how it should work. Would it help if I put together a one-pager with your unit count and
> the actual numbers so you're not doing that from memory? …And what's their name — I'd rather they
> hear it from you first than get cold-called by me."

**I. "We tried valet trash before and it was a disaster."** *(concede immediately)*
> "Then it was a bad company and you were right to kill it. What actually went wrong — was it missed
> nights, or trash sitting out? …Yeah. That's the exact thing the nightly log is for; you'd see a
> miss the next morning instead of hearing it from a resident three days later."

**J. "Who pays when a resident wrecks a can?"**
> "Forty dollars, and it goes on your invoice — you'd collect it from the resident the same way
> you'd handle any damage. Normal wear and tear is on me, and if my guy breaks it, that's mine."

**K. "What about recycling?"**
> "I can do it — two bins per unit, seven dollars a door on top. Only thing is I need a two-year
> commitment on the recycling side, because that's a second set of containers and a second stop."

**L. "You're a one-man operation. What if you get sick?"**
> "It's not just me — my people are W-2 employees, not me subcontracting it out, and that's the point.
> But I'd rather you hold me to the contract than my promise: missing nights is a default in there,
> you give me fifteen days to fix it, and if I don't, you're out."

## OPERATIONAL QUESTIONS — the ones that catch you bluffing *(reference)*

Not objections — competence checks. A manager asking these is **interested**. Answer plainly, and
**when the answer is no, say no.**

**"What time do your guys come?"**
> "Evenings we're running roughly six to ten, mornings we start early and we're done before most
> people leave for work. We'd set the exact window with you and tell your residents in writing."

**"What if a resident doesn't put their can out?"**
> "Nothing happens — we're not knocking. If they put out loose trash or something that doesn't
> belong, my guy photographs it and it shows up in your log instead of me arguing with your resident."

**"What if my compactor is full?"**
> "Then we can't run that night — it's in the contract. You tell me it's emptied and we're back the
> next scheduled pickup. I'm not going to leave a pile next to a full compactor and call that
> service."

**"What happens in an ice storm?"** ← **Don't overpromise. Say the real term.**
> "We don't run in bad weather, and that's written in. I'm not putting my guys on your stairs in
> ice. You'd hear from me that night, not the next morning."

**"Does this feed into Yardi / RealPage / Entrata?"** ← **Do not bluff this. It doesn't.**
> "No, and I won't tell you otherwise. You get an invoice and a per-unit backup file every month
> that ties out to your rent roll. If re-keying that is a dealbreaker, tell me now and I'll save us
> both the time."

**"Who do my residents call when something's wrong?"**
> "They can request a comeback right in the app themselves, which is most of it. Anything past that
> comes to me, not to your office. That's kind of the whole point."

**"Who cleans the cans when somebody moves out?"**
> "That one's on you in the contract — your turn crew handles the container the same as the unit. I
> replace them when they're actually worn out."

**"Are you insured? Can you name us as additional insured?"**
> "One million per occurrence, two million aggregate, plus workers comp on every employee. I'll send
> the certificate today and we'll get you named on it."

**"How fast could you start?"** ← **Do not say "immediately." Know your real number.**
> "Realistically two to three weeks — containers have to be ordered and staged, and I'd rather stage
> them right than start late on night one."

**"How many properties can you actually handle?"** ← **NEVER say unlimited.**
> "I'm being deliberate about that. I'd rather run four properties perfectly than twelve badly, and
> the month-to-month means I have to. If I couldn't staff you properly I'd tell you no."

## WHEN IT'S NOT A FIT — the disqualify *(reference — this is a WIN)*

Three situations. Say it plainly and go.

**Managed by a national with a preferred vendor:**
> "Ah — then you're probably locked into whoever they've got nationally, and I'd just be wasting your
> afternoon. If that ever changes, here's my card."

**Under 100 units:**
> "Honestly, at that size the route doesn't work for me and I'd end up cutting corners on you. I'd
> rather tell you that than take the contract and do it badly."

**They charge residents $25 and want a price fight:**
> "At twenty-five you'd only be keeping seven a door with me, and there are guys who'll do it
> cheaper than I will. If what you want is the lowest number, that's not me — and I'd rather say so
> than waste your time."

**Log all three as `disqualified — revisit`, not as losses.** Small properties grow, managers change
companies, and national agreements get renegotiated. The manager who remembers you told them the
truth is the one who calls you from their next property.

## NEVER SAY (compliance / brand)

- ❌ **Any property count or reference.** You have none. The honest answer in Objection E is stronger
  than anything you could invent, and inventing one ends you the first time somebody asks to call them.
- ❌ **"$20 for a lost can."** It's **$40**, that's what the contract says, and a manager will catch
  the mismatch when legal reads it.
- ❌ **Yardi / RealPage / Entrata integration.** We're not eligible to build one. See
  `brain/decisions.md` (2026-08-21).
- ❌ **"As many units as you want."** With W-2 staff, hiring is the constraint. Overpromising capacity
  and then missing nights on your first property is how this business dies in month three.
- ❌ **Any promise of service during inclement weather** or on a listed holiday.
- ❌ **Any guaranteed NOI, retention, or resident-satisfaction number.**
- ❌ **Badmouthing the incumbent by name.** Ask questions and let the manager say it. When *you* say
  it, you're a salesman; when *they* say it, it's a fact they now own.
- ❌ **Pitching the leasing agent as the buyer.** Get the manager's name and come back.
- ❌ **A feature monologue.** Ever. Two things, then stop.
